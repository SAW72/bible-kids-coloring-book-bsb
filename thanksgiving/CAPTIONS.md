## Page 7 — `pages/thanksgiving/07-first-harvest.png`

Caption:

```
In the fall of 1621, the colonists gathered a good harvest of Indian corn.
Give thanks to the LORD, for He is good. — Psalm 136:1 (BSB)
```

History: Edward Winslow's letter of December 11, 1621, printed in *Mourt's Relation*, says the harvest was in and that they had a good increase of Indian corn. Pilgrim Hall Museum quotes that letter for the 1621 harvest and the feast that followed it. The page states the harvest. It does not quote Winslow, because the modern transcripts differ in spelling from the 1622 printing.

https://www.pilgrimhall.org/thanksgiving-national-day-of-mourning/evolution-of-an-american-holiday/

Verse: The line is the opening clause of Psalm 136:1 in the Berean Standard Bible. The full verse continues, "His loving devotion endures forever."

https://biblehub.com/bsb/psalms/136.htm

## Page 8 — `pages/thanksgiving/08-harvest-feast.png`

Caption:

```
In the fall of 1621, Massasoit and about ninety Wampanoag men joined the colonists for a three-day harvest feast.
```

Source: Edward Winslow, letter of December 11, 1621, as quoted by Pilgrim Hall Museum from *Mourt's Relation*. Winslow wrote that Massasoit came "with some nintie men, whom for three days we entertained and feasted," and that they brought five deer. The page uses "about ninety" for Winslow's "nintie" and does not add foods he did not name.

https://www.pilgrimhall.org/thanksgiving-national-day-of-mourning/evolution-of-an-american-holiday/

## Page 9 — `pages/thanksgiving/09-pilgrims-prayer.png`

Caption:

```
In the summer of 1623, after a drought, Plymouth kept a day of thanksgiving and prayer.
Give thanks to the LORD, for He is good; His loving devotion endures forever. — Psalm 107:1 (BSB)
```

History: Plimoth Patuxet Museums, "A Day of Thanksgiving, Summer 1623." In the summer of 1623 a drought threatened the corn. The colony held a day of humiliation and prayer. Rain followed, and the colony then kept a day of thanksgiving. Edward Winslow tells this in *Good Newes from New England* (1624). The page does not give a day of the month, because the 1624 account does not.

https://plimoth.org/yath/unit-4/a-day-of-thanksgiving-summer-1623

Verse: Psalm 107:1, Berean Standard Bible, full verse.

https://biblehub.com/bsb/psalms/107.htm

## Page 10 — `pages/thanksgiving/10-washington.png`

Caption:

```
On October 3, 1789, President George Washington proclaimed a day of public thanksgiving.
He assigned “Thursday the 26th day of November next.”
```

Source: George Washington, Thanksgiving Proclamation, 3 October 1789, National Archives, Founders Online. The proclamation says: "Now therefore I do recommend and assign Thursday the 26th day of November next to be devoted by the People of these States to the service of that great and glorious Being..." It is dated "at the City of New-York the third day of October in the year of our Lord 1789." The quoted words on the page are that exact phrase.

https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Washington/05-04-02-0091

## Page 11 — `pages/thanksgiving/11-lincoln.png`

Caption:

```
On October 3, 1863, President Abraham Lincoln proclaimed a national day of Thanksgiving.
He called it “a day of Thanksgiving and Praise.”
```

Source: Abraham Lincoln, Presidential Proclamation 106, October 3, 1863, as transcribed by the National Archives. The proclamation says the president invites the people "to set apart and observe the last Thursday of November next, as a day of Thanksgiving and Praise to our beneficent Father who dwelleth in the Heavens." The quoted words on the page are that exact phrase. The page does not say Congress made Thanksgiving a legal holiday in 1863. A later statute, in 1941, fixed the federal legal holiday on the fourth Thursday in November.

https://education.blogs.archives.gov/2020/11/22/lincolns-thanksgiving-proclamation/
